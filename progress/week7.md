# Week 2 Progress

## What Has Been Built

The core quantitative pipeline is complete end-to-end. The data layer ingests 1-minute OHLCV bars for 100 large-cap US equities across 11 GICS sectors (Technology, Financials, Healthcare, Energy, and others) via the EODHD API, resamples them to 15-minute bars, and stores them as per-ticker Parquet files. A centralised configuration system (`utils/config.py`) encodes the full universe, sector mapping, and all tunable parameters as typed dataclasses, making it straightforward to swap settings without touching module code. On top of the data layer, the pair-selection module (`analysis/cointegration.py`) runs the Engle-Granger two-step procedure on within-sector pairs over a rolling formation window: OLS regression to estimate the hedge ratio, followed by an ADF stationarity test on the residual spread, with pairs filtered to p-value ≤ 0.05 and half-lives between 5 and 120 trading observations. Passing pairs are ranked by p-value and assigned inverse-rank signal weights. The signal module (`analysis/signals.py`) then takes these pairs and intraday price data, computes rolling z-scores of the spread, and runs a stateful entry/exit state machine that generates +1/−1/0 position signals per bar, with configurable entry threshold, mean-reversion exit, stop-loss exit, and time-stop exit.

With the signal infrastructure in place, a parallelised grid search (`notebooks/parameter_search.ipynb`, `scripts/grid_worker.py`) was built to evaluate parameter combinations — varying z-score window length, entry/exit/stop thresholds, maximum holding time, and formation window — over a representative H2 2022 sample period. Performance is measured via mean net return per bar in position (after 80 bps round-trip cost) and Spearman information coefficient. To make the search tractable, cointegration results are pre-computed and cached by (formation window, trading day) before the grid loop, eliminating redundant Parquet reads; workers are dispatched via `multiprocessing` with `spawn` start method to avoid a fork thread-pool incompatibility in Polars. Analysis of the results, written up as annotated notebook cells, shows that z_entry is the dominant parameter (Pearson r = +0.33 with mean net return): entry thresholds below 2.0 are unprofitable after transaction costs at all tested configurations, while thresholds of 2.5–3.0 consistently yield positive net returns and positive IC. The recommended parameter set for the full backtest is z_entry = 3.0, zscore_window = 10 days, formation window = 42 days, and max holding = 60 minutes, selected from the feasible set where IC > 0 and the strategy is robust to minor parameter perturbations.

## Challenge: From Signal Evaluation to Full P&L Backtest

The grid search evaluates signals by computing a forward return for every bar where a position is active, then averaging across bars and holding horizons. This approach is computationally fast and useful for parameter ranking, but it is a biased proxy for actual trading profitability. A bar that sits mid-trade in a profitable reversal contributes a positive observation even though no new capital is deployed at that point; as a result, high-frequency parameter combinations (low z_entry, short zscore_window) accumulate many positive observations during winning multi-bar holds and appear better than they are. The next step is implementing a genuine trade-level P&L backtest: simulating entry and exit execution prices, tracking concurrent open positions across pairs (capped at the configured `max_pairs = 10`), computing realized P&L per trade rather than per bar, and aggregating to daily and cumulative equity curves. This requires careful handling of overlapping signals within the same pair, position sizing from the $100,000 capital base, and proper application of the 4-leg round-trip cost only at trade open and close rather than at every bar. Beyond the mechanics, the deeper challenge is that all results to date are in-sample on a single six-month bear market period (H2 2022). Hypothesis 4 (out-of-sample degradation) and Hypothesis 6 (walk-forward robustness) remain untested; the backtest must be designed from the outset with a held-out test set and rolling walk-forward windows to give any confidence that the chosen parameters generalise beyond the evaluation period.
